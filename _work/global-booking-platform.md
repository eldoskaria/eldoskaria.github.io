## Context

OET's global booking platform handles more than 15,000 paper-based and 3,000 computer-based assessments a month across 40+ countries. It's a regulated system with three very different groups depending on it: individual candidates, enterprise partners and internal operations teams.

## The problem

- Rescheduling a booking needed an agent to step in and run manual scripts, which meant back-and-forth with customer service for every change.
- Timezone resolution relied on Google Timezone APIs, which cost more than it needed to and wasn't as accurate as it could be for candidates in some locations.
- Pricing was the same across booking products, leaving commercial growth on the table.

## My role

I own the platform roadmap and drive continuous improvement, balancing long-term platform health against a steady stream of near-term delivery.

## What I did

### Self-service rescheduling
Introduced a "reschedule on demand" feature so candidates can move their own bookings. Agents no longer run rescheduling scripts by hand.

### Timezone API migration
Moved timezone resolution from Google Timezone APIs to LocationIQ, cutting API costs and improving localisation accuracy for candidates in 40+ countries.

### Differential pricing
Introduced differential pricing across booking products, which drove a 20% increase in global OET campaign sales.

### Booking experience
Redesigned the booking journey for paper-based tests, cutting booking time by 20%.

## What made it hard

The platform never stops: every change lands on a live, global, high-stakes system. Keeping it healthy over the long term while shipping continuously is the core of the job.

## Outcomes

- **+20%** global OET campaign sales from differential pricing.
- **−20%** booking time for paper-based test takers.
- Manual rescheduling scripts eliminated through self-service.
- Lower timezone API costs with better localisation accuracy.
